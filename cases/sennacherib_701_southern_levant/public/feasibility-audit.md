# Feasibility Audit

## Decision

**GO for strict preregistration and non-map evidence collection; NO-GO for reconstruction at this stage.** The case can be bounded as the conventionally dated **701 BCE campaign/disposition horizon**. That horizon includes the third-campaign operations and dispositions directly reported as their consequences. It is not a day-level political snapshot.

The audit used the entire fixed allowance: 12 queries and 10 text/catalog access attempts. Six preliminary research sources and one metadata-only held-out candidate were admitted. No polygon or spatial input was created.

## Gate result

All feasibility thresholds pass. Judah, Ekron, Ashdod, Gaza and Ashkelon have explicit text paths. Ashkelon has its royal seat plus Bīt-Daganna, Joppa, Banayabarqa and Azuru in the Ṣidqâ relation; Judah has Jerusalem plus a Lachish textual/archaeological path, although Lachish is not yet an admitted local-control point. Twelve named spatial candidates are locked: Joppa, Ekron, Lachish, Jerusalem, Ashdod, Gaza, Ashkelon, Bīt-Daganna/Beth-Dagon, Banayabarqa/Bene-Baraq, Azuru, Eltekeh and Timnah.

Five potentially distinct source lineages are available: the Assyrian royal-inscription tradition; the Lachish gate excavation; the southwest-Judah multi-excavation critique; the Tel Miqne-Ekron excavation/local-inscription tradition; and the Deuteronomistic Kings textual tradition. This is a feasibility count, not a claim that they independently confirm the same event. ORACC Q003496 and Q003945 remain one partisan royal lineage.

## Evidence and rights

ORACC supplies line-addressable identifiers and translations for the third-campaign reports; its annotated edition states CC BY-SA 3.0. The IAA Lachish record is DOI-addressed and its abstract reports the 701 destruction evidence. The BASOR abstract supplies direct counterevidence against assigning every late-eighth-century destruction to 701. Harvard’s Ekron record supplies later excavation and local-inscription context, not direct 701 control. STEP provides a verse-addressable path to 2 Kings 18:13–17; its textual layer, composition and relationship to Assyrian traditions remain unresolved, and translation rights limit use to citation and paraphrase.

The admitted source bodies are not copied into the repository. `source-access-rights.json` records access scope, locator, rights and lineage for each source.

## Political ontology

Local dynastic rule, contested local authority, transfer, imperial suzerainty, tribute, military presence, siege/conquest, destruction and local administration are separate layers. The current bundle admits display or exclusion decisions only. Assyrian intervention at Ekron and tribute imposed on Padî do not establish direct Assyrian municipal administration. The capture of Judahite towns does not define Assyrian local control. Archaeological destruction at Lachish identifies an event horizon, not a territorial assignment.

Two exclusions are already load-bearing. The towns transferred to Mitinti, Padî and Ṣilli-Bēl are unnamed and cannot become invented anchors. Regional late-eighth-century destruction levels cannot all be absorbed into the 701 campaign without site-specific dating. The Hebrew Bible path cannot count as independent corroboration until its textual and source relationship is reviewed.

## Held-out audit

*The Carta Bible Atlas* (2011, ISBN 978-965-220-814-9) is registered from bibliographic metadata only. Its comprehensive scope makes expression of at least eight of the twenty frozen units plausible; exact compatibility remains sealed and may prove inconclusive.

A WorldCat/search result exposed a front-cover thumbnail for the *Oxford Bible Atlas*, so that candidate is excluded. Unrequested results for *The Bible Atlas* exposed place-label and campaign-sequence summaries; that work is also excluded. No linked atlas page or PDF was opened.

## Real limits

No candidate entity yet has two independent, horizon-applicable named-locality **local-administration** relations. Ashdod and Gaza receive unnamed towns. Several ancient-to-modern identifications, especially Azuru, require a reviewed gazetteer or site record. Indexed ORACC lines were accessible while several direct page fetches failed. The primary comparator’s exact 701 content is unknown by design. Automated validation can audit repository artifacts and declarations; it cannot prove a person’s complete browsing history.
