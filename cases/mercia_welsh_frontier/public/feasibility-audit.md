# Feasibility Audit

## Decision

**Conditional GO for preregistration and evidence collection; NO-GO for reconstruction at this stage.** The 780-01-01 through 796-07-29 interval and the Rhuddlan–upper Severn corridor are precise enough to freeze. Mercia is directly documented in-window. Powys and Gwynedd have named documentary pathways but not yet enough interval-local control evidence to enter a solver.

## Spatial evidence supply

The audit located twelve candidates: Rhuddlan; Ffordd Llanfynydd/Treuddyn; Coedpoeth; Cadwgan Hall–River Clywedog; Chirk Castle/Home Farm; Plas Offa at Chirk; Trefonen; Llanymynech Hill; Newcastle-on-Clun; Rushock Hill; Old Oswestry/Wat's Dyke; and the Pillar of Eliseg/Bwlch yr Oernant route. They cover events, engineering sections, route and terrain context, chronology counterevidence, and an alternative dyke. None is admitted as territorial control.

Stable locators include *Annales Cambriae* A-text fol. 192a col. 1 entries a344, a357 and a359; Asser chapter 14, print pp. 8–9; Electronic Sawyer S 120; Coflein NPRNs 306866, 275792 and 275756; NHLE 1014899; ADS record 1227734; and the UCL Pillar of Eliseg summary. Access and rights are recorded in `source-access-rights.json`.

## Held-out audit

David H. Hill's *An Atlas of Anglo-Saxon England* (1981, ISBN 0-631-11181-6) is the primary sealed candidate; Christopher Daniell's *Atlas of Medieval Britain* (2013, ISBN 978-0-415-60223-5) is the fallback. Only ADS/publisher bibliographic metadata was used. No candidate map body was viewed. A Shepherd atlas candidate was excluded because a search engine exposed a textual label summary.

## Real blockers

Welsh polity continuity and local control remain underdetermined; Rhuddlan's participants and consequence are unstated in the annal; Chirk dating does not securely establish Offan construction; surviving sections do not establish a political function. The case can test whether the workflow stops honestly when evidence remains insufficient. It cannot yet support a polygon.
